# Smarter Justice Public Paid Human Review Standard

Version: 1.0.0  
Approved development baseline: Smarter Justice v1.7.4  
Current operating status: Paused — no live public charges authorized

## Purpose

Smarter Justice may offer separately priced Human Review Specialist services while preserving meaningful free AI-guided public starting help. Human Review Specialist work is an optional operational service. It is not legal advice, tax advice, accounting advice, representation, a professional engagement, a filing service, or a guarantee of completeness, acceptance, eligibility, deadlines, outcomes, or professional availability.

This standard applies to Smarter Justice and every participating micro-portal. A portal may narrow or omit services when specialty, jurisdiction, risk, capacity, privacy, or professional-responsibility concerns require it. A portal may not weaken these minimum safeguards.

## Required separation

Every public page, checkout, receipt, dashboard, and handoff must distinguish:

1. Free AI-guided starting help.
2. Optional public supporter or continuity membership, if later approved.
3. Human Review Specialist service charges.
4. Separately engaged attorney, CPA, enrolled agent, accountant, or other professional fees.
5. Court, government, filing, tax, premium, delivery, translation, expert, and other third-party costs.

Paying for Human Review Specialist work does not create an attorney-client, accountant-client, tax-practitioner, fiduciary, agency, or representative relationship.

## Permitted launch catalog

The initial catalog may include only clearly scoped operational review services such as:

- starting-information completeness review;
- notice and deadline organization review;
- form and supporting-document organization review; and
- preparation for a separately engaged professional review.

The public catalog must not sell “attorney review,” “tax professional review,” legal advice, representation, filing, negotiation, court appearance, or another licensed service as Human Review Specialist work.

## Service-record requirements

A service cannot become billable unless its owner-controlled record contains:

- approved service name and identifier;
- public scope and exclusions;
- positive approved price;
- turnaround or service window;
- revision or follow-up policy;
- current terms version;
- billing-active status;
- support ownership;
- refund or cancellation policy status; and
- portal applicability and capacity limits where relevant.

Changing one field must not silently activate a service whose other requirements are incomplete.

## User acknowledgments

Before checkout, the user must affirm that:

- the service scope and exclusions are understood;
- the service is not professional advice or representation;
- professional, government, court, and third-party fees are separate;
- the disclosed cancellation/refund policy is accepted; and
- electronic service communications are accepted.

The order must record the accepted terms version and acknowledgment state.

## Fail-closed activation gate

Public checkout remains closed unless all required conditions agree, including:

- the production public-paid-services kill switch is on;
- the owner operating status is approved;
- live charges are explicitly allowed;
- the service itself is approved and billing-active;
- current scope, price, turnaround, revision policy, and terms exist;
- required support and refund operations are approved;
- sensitive-traffic approval is present when required;
- production persistence and protected storage are appropriate for the data handled;
- authenticated service email and owner escalation channels are configured;
- Stripe products/prices and signed webhook handling are configured and tested;
- duplicate checkout and webhook protections are active;
- monitoring, incident, cancellation, refund, and reconciliation procedures exist; and
- Roger has explicitly approved activation.

A price ID, environment variable, page button, successful code test, or Stripe account alone is not launch approval.

## Order lifecycle

The controlled lifecycle must keep payment, fulfillment, support, and refund state separately visible. Supported states may include:

- checkout created;
- payment pending;
- paid and awaiting assignment;
- assigned;
- in review;
- awaiting user information;
- delivered;
- revision requested;
- completed;
- canceled;
- refund pending;
- refunded;
- payment failed; and
- disputed or held.

Every owner/staff mutation must be authorized, validated, auditable, and protected against duplicate actions. User-facing status must not expose private staff notes, security details, internal risk flags, or other customers’ information.

## Fulfillment controls

Human Review Specialist operations require:

- named operational owner;
- trained and authorized staff;
- capacity and assignment controls;
- target turnaround monitoring;
- missing-information workflow;
- delivery evidence;
- revision handling;
- complaint and escalation handling;
- immediate pause capability; and
- referral to an independent qualified professional when the request exceeds permitted scope.

Staff must not provide legal conclusions, strategy, tax positions, professional opinions, predictions, or representation unless separately qualified, engaged, and authorized through the appropriate professional workflow.

## Cancellation, refund, and payment controls

Before live charges:

- the public cancellation/refund policy must be approved and versioned;
- users must be told when work is considered started;
- partial, full, duplicate, failed, disputed, and owner-initiated refunds must be operationally tested;
- Stripe events must reconcile to internal order state;
- duplicate checkouts and duplicate webhook events must not create duplicate fulfillment or revenue;
- support must have authority and instructions for common refund situations; and
- receipts and confirmations must clearly identify the purchased Human Review Specialist service.

Do not describe a payment as a charitable donation or tax-deductible contribution.

## Privacy and safety

Collect only the information needed to deliver the purchased service. Confidential or sensitive documents remain disabled until protected production storage, access controls, retention, deletion, audit, incident, and recovery evidence are approved. Public kiosk or street staff must not solicit sensitive oral facts where others can hear.

Emergency, domestic-violence, medical, criminal, immigration, deadline, or other high-risk situations require prominent appropriate-resource and professional-help boundaries. Smarter Justice and Human Review Specialists are not emergency responders, hotlines, shelters, courts, government agencies, or law firms.

## Owner Control Center requirements

The private Owner Control Center must show:

- master activation controls;
- every service’s readiness reasons;
- approved prices, scope, turnaround, revision policy, and terms version;
- order, payment, assignment, fulfillment, support, cancellation, refund, dispute, and reconciliation state;
- capacity, overdue, exception, and incident indicators;
- exportable audit evidence; and
- the distinction among code complete, automated tests passed, exact artifact tested, deployed, live verified, operationally accepted, and public-service activation approved.

## Micro-portal adoption

Every micro-portal continuation prompt and build list must identify:

- which Human Review Specialist services are adopted, narrowed, or omitted;
- portal-specific scope and exclusions;
- privacy and sensitive-data boundaries;
- current terms and pricing versions;
- staff capacity and escalation ownership;
- automated and real-device test evidence;
- deployment and live-verification status; and
- whether public checkout remains paused.

No portal may create a duplicate or incompatible payment, order, refund, or professional-engagement model without an owner-approved documented deviation.

## Launch evidence

Public Human Review Specialist charges may open only after exact-artifact testing plus real evidence for production persistence, protected storage where needed, authenticated email, signed Stripe webhook handling, complete test-payment lifecycle, support/refund operations, monitoring, incident response, legal and professional-responsibility review, real-device acceptance, named staff capacity, and explicit owner approval.

Until those conditions are satisfied, public service pages may explain the model and scope, but checkout must remain fail-closed.
